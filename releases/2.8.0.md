## What this release is about

The biggest change is **portal managers**. A manager can now sign in to the self-service portal and see, reply to and close their team's tickets. Their team comes from the Manager field you already keep on each person, plus any people, groups or departments you add. It was asked for in [discussion #62](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/discussions/62). Managers are off until you switch them on.

Before managers could exist, some tickets had to be kept private, so tickets can now be marked **confidential**. A confidential ticket is hidden from managers by default. It is never sent to AI, and only a bare outline of it leaves FreeITSM through webhooks or trackers. Each ticket also records who has looked at it.

**Feature Bingo** is a new System page with a card for each of 572 features. A star lights on each card once that feature is set up on your install, which shows you the parts of FreeITSM you have not found yet.

You can also choose **how each team signs in**, as asked for in [#41](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/issues/41).

## New features

- **Portal managers.** Set them up on the new **System → Managers** page, where they are off by default. From there you choose:
  - whether the Manager field counts, and whether it gives direct reports only or the whole reporting chain
  - whether managers may reply and close
  - how much of a confidential ticket they see: nothing, an outline, or everything
  - whether people who have left stay visible
  - who may set up management lines

  The page lists every manager with how many people they can see. It also flags managers who have left, and department lines that match nobody. You can check the list before switching managers on. Managers only ever reach people in their own company.

- **Manager access page.** Open it from **Manager access** on Tickets → Users, Assets → Users or System → Managers. It has three panels: people to add, what gives the manager access, and who they can see. Click anyone in the third panel to see why the manager can see them, drawn out step by step. You can add a person, a people group, a department, everyone or a reporting line, and you can exclude anyone. An exclusion always wins.

- **Team tickets in the portal.** Managers get a Team tickets list that loads 50 tickets at a time and can be filtered by person and status. Counts cover the whole team.

- **Tell managers about new tickets.** System → Managers can email managers when one of their team raises a ticket, or put it in their portal notification bell. It is off by default. A manager is only told about tickets they are allowed to open, and never about a confidential one.

- **A notification bell in the portal.** It is the same bell analysts have. Every manager has it, and other portal users see it once they have a notification.

- **Confidential tickets.** Analysts set this in the ticket's Sensitivity field. Requesters can tick **This is confidential** when raising a ticket, or mark one of their own tickets confidential later. A department or a mailbox can make all its new tickets confidential. Confidential tickets show a padlock. The setting is also available through the REST API.

- **Confidential tickets are not sent to AI.** Every AI feature refuses a confidential ticket, and features that look across many tickets leave them out. You can change this in **Tickets → Settings → General → Confidential tickets and AI**. Webhooks, Slack and Teams posts for a confidential ticket carry only the ticket's number, status, priority, department, team and dates. Escalating one to Jira or Azure DevOps asks you to confirm first, and workflows skip it.

- **Who has seen a ticket.** Opening a ticket is recorded as a **Viewed** line in the ticket's Audit, once per person per day. In the portal, the requester sees **Who has seen this ticket**, which lists other portal users such as their manager. It does not list the service desk.

- **Sign-in method per team.** **System → Teams** can set a sign-in method for a whole team, such as password or a particular SSO provider. Analysts follow their team unless you set theirs by hand. If an analyst's teams disagree, a **Sign-in conflict** button explains the conflict and gives three ways to fix it.

- **Feature Bingo.** It is the first card on the System page and covers 24 modules. Each card explains what the feature is, why it is worth setting up and exactly what lights the star, with a **Take me there** link to the right page. You can search and filter by module, category (AI, Security, Automation and more) and tier (Essential, Recommended, Extra). **Not for us** takes a card, or a whole module, out of the score. You can add cards of your own in a folder that updates never touch. The wiki explains how.

## Improvements

- **One person editor.** Tickets → Users and Assets → Users now open the same editor. The Manager field is a type-ahead search, which helps with large organisations.
- **Your System name is used in page titles.** The name set in **Tickets → Settings → General** now appears in browser tabs. Before, it was saved but never used.
- **"Powered by FreeITSM"** now appears, very small and faint, at the foot of the staff and portal sign-in pages.
- **Links to Tickets → Settings open the right tab**, for example `settings/#sla`.
- **Help and wiki** cover managers, confidential tickets and Feature Bingo.
- **This release's new screens are English-only in the other thirteen languages for now.** They fall back to English cleanly and will be translated in the next batch.

## Fixes

- **Links in emails and calendar feeds could use the wrong http or https.** They now follow the same rule as sign-in behind a reverse proxy (2.7.1).
- **Deleting an SSO provider could lock out its analysts.** The password form refused them, and there was no SSO button for them either.
- **Editing a person on Assets → Users could clear their manager** if the manager was in a company you could not see.
- **Editing a person on Assets → Users could fail on Save.** This happened to analysts who had Assets but not Tickets.
- **With the portal logo shown, the bottom of New Ticket, My Tickets and the course player could not be reached.**
- **The Analysts help said SSO analysts could still use their password.** They cannot, and never could.

## Upgrading

1. Pull the new code, or rebuild the Docker image.
2. Sign in as an administrator and run **System → Database Verification**. This adds the tables for managers, confidential tickets, ticket views, portal notifications and Feature Bingo. Managers and the Confidential setting stay unavailable until it has run.
3. Hard-refresh your browser (Ctrl-F5).

Nothing changes for your portal users until you switch managers on in System → Managers. The **Before you switch on** checklist in System → Help → Managers is worth reading first.

## Rollback

Safe - nothing was removed from the database, so you can go back to 2.7.1 with the code alone. Tickets marked confidential will show as normal again in 2.7.1.
