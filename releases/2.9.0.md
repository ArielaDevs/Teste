## What this release is about

This release adds a whole new module, **Domains**, asked for in [#154](https://github.com/edmozley/freeitsm/issues/154). It keeps a list of every domain name your organisation holds, or that each of your clients holds. Add a domain and FreeITSM looks it up at its registry and fills in the registrar and the registration and expiry dates. It then checks the domain's DNS, email security and certificates and gives it a grade from A+ to F, with plain advice on what to fix. From then on it keeps watching: it tells you before a domain or its certificate expires, and straight away if something changes that looks like somebody has taken it over.

## New features

- **Domains.** A new module in the launcher, with these parts:
  - **Register.** Each domain with a countdown to expiry, its status, registrar, owner, security grade, locks and certificate. Ready-made views answer the usual questions: what needs attention, what expires this month, and which domains can be transferred away.
  - **Adding domains.** Add one at a time, paste a whole list, or import a spreadsheet saved as CSV. You match the spreadsheet's columns to FreeITSM's fields on screen. New domains are looked up and checked while you watch.
  - **Automatic details.** The registrar, the registration and expiry dates, the name servers and whether transfers are locked are all filled in from the registry. For the few domain endings that publish no expiry date, such as .de, you enter it once by hand.
  - **Security grade.** Every domain is checked daily and graded A+ to F. The checks cover its locks, DNSSEC and the records that stop other people sending email as you (SPF, DKIM and DMARC), and the certificates on the website. Each problem comes with advice in plain English. A domain you never send email from, such as a parked one, is checked for being locked down against spoofing instead.
  - **Change detection.** If the name servers, mail servers or registrar change, or a lock comes off, the change is recorded and can be emailed at once. This is what a hijacked domain looks like.
  - **Watching for copycats.** Two optional checks. One flags new certificates issued for your names. The other finds look-alike domains registered one typo away from yours, especially ones that can receive email.
  - **Alerts.** Renewal and certificate reminders are sent as one digest per person. They go to each domain's owner, to a shared list, or both. Each reminder is sent once per expiry date, and FreeITSM can also raise a renewal task or ticket. Email is off until you switch it on, and **Preview** shows who would be emailed first.
  - **Accounts and auth codes.** Record which registrar account holds each domain, who owns it and who has the second sign-in factor. Auth codes are stored encrypted, only people you allow can see them, and every look is recorded.
  - **Dashboard.** The next twelve months of renewals and what they will cost, how the grades are spread, and email security across all your domains.
  - **Table view.** Every column, sortable and filterable, with saved views and CSV export.
- **Domains elsewhere in FreeITSM.** A Domains card on **Watchtower**, expiry dates in the **Calendar** (if you choose), notifications for a domain's owner, workflow triggers for every domain event, domains in **search**, hover previews, attached documents, and a list of the domains each registrar holds on its supplier page in **Contracts**.
- **REST API: Domains.** Endpoints to list, add, update and delete domains, look one up at its registry, re-run its checks, and read its findings and history. Auth codes are never returned by the API.

## Improvements

- **Demo data** now includes eight sample domains across every grade, so you can look around the module before adding your own.

## Upgrading

1. Pull the new code, or rebuild the Docker image.
2. Sign in as an administrator and run **System → Database Verification**. It creates the Domains tables.
3. Hard-refresh your browser (Ctrl-F5).

The new Domains screens are in English only in this release. The other languages will follow.

To keep domains up to date without anybody opening the module, schedule `cron/domains.php` to run hourly. **Domains → Settings → Monitoring** shows the exact command. Without it, Domains still refreshes itself, but only when somebody opens it. Analysts who can use every module see Domains straight away; anyone else needs it granted in **System → Modules**, like any new module.

## Rollback

Safe - this release only adds to the database, so you can go back to 2.8.0.
